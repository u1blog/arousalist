How it works

Three simple steps

🧭
Try it

Each experience has clear instructions — equipment, what to do, and how to approach it solo or with a partner. Go at your own pace.

📋
Rate it

After each experience, give it a rating from ✗ to ✓✓ — separately for solo and partner versions, and for giver and receiver roles where relevant.

🔍
Find your profile

Look for patterns. Sensation-led or dynamic-led? Giver or receiver? Solo curious or drawn to the partner dynamic? The answers accumulate.

The rating system

Four honest options

No right answers. The goal is accurate self-knowledge — so a ✗ is as useful as a ✓✓.

Experience tiers

Solo, together, or both

Each experience is flagged for how well it works solo versus with a partner. The solo version isn't just a fallback — it gives you useful baseline information.

🧍
Solo start

Try this alone first to understand your baseline response before involving a partner.

🧍→👫
Better with a partner

The solo version is useful groundwork, but a partner unlocks the real experience — unpredictability, the relational dynamic, someone else in control.

👫
Partner only

The dynamic between two people is the point. These experiences don't really work alone — the interaction itself is what you're exploring.

A note on consent and safety

Every experience involving a partner should begin with a clear conversation about what you're trying, what you want to pay attention to, and how you'll communicate if something isn't working. Agree on a signal or word to pause or stop — and use it.

This is a guide for curious adults exploring consensually. Go slowly, pay attention to your body, and don't push through anything that feels genuinely uncomfortable. The purpose is self-knowledge, not endurance.
